description: The room link, letting people in, and talking to the team without the agent hearing it.

## Share a session

There are three ways to let people reach your host, chosen in the client: `F2` → Session →
*Who can join*.

| Way | Who can join | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| **This machine** | only you | nothing — or start with `--local` |
| **Same network** | people on your Wi-Fi, office network or VPN | nothing. The invite link carries your network address, and the host listens on every interface |
| **Over the internet** (default) | anyone you let in | nothing: the Plural relay (`relay.plrl.host`), or the address of your own with `--relay` |

With *Over the internet* the host connects out to the relay and so does every guest — nobody
opens a port, which is what makes it work from a home network. The relay only routes frames: it
never decides permissions and never stores content.

## Through a tunnel

A tunnel gives your host a public address without a server of your own, with one command:

```bash
plural --tunnel cloudflared   # needs the cloudflared program, no account; address changes each start
plural --tunnel ngrok         # needs the ngrok program and a free account
```

The first lines of the conversation show an invite whose link carries the tunnel address
(`wss://…`); when you quit, the tunnel stops with the host. The tunnel provider terminates TLS,
so it can read the traffic — trust it like you would a relay — and your host port is reachable
from the internet while it is up. Share the link only with the people you mean, and end it with
`/invite revoke` if it leaks.

## Join a session

```bash
plural join "ws://host:7777/r_…?invite=…" --name alia
```

## Let people in

The link at the top of the conversation is the **room link**. It is permanent: it never
expires, it works for any number of people and it stays the same when you resume the session
(while the address of your host stays the same). You can send it at any time.

Anyone who opens it waits on a screen that says the host has to let them in. You get a card in
the list of things that wait for you, with their name and the program they use, and a bell if
you have it on:

| Key | Answer |
|---|---|
| `y` or `enter` | let them in as a participant |
| `v` | let them in as a viewer |
| `n` | refuse |

Until you answer they see nothing of the room. A request that nobody answers lapses after two
minutes, at most five people can wait at once, and a room holds twenty people. After you let
someone in they come back on their own for 24 hours without asking again.

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `/invite` | copy the room link |
| `/invite once`, `/invite viewer` | one-time invites for one person, who go in without your answer: a participant or a viewer, valid for an hour |
| `/invite revoke` | end the room link: those waiting are refused, the old link stops working and the new one is copied. People who are already in stay |
| `/invite open` | anyone with the link goes in at once, for a team you trust. The link is then as good as a password, keep it private |
| `/invite approve` | back to letting each person in (the default) |

The same choice is in `F2` → Session → *People who use the room link*. A host started by a
script can use `--admission open` (or `PLURAL_ADMISSION=open`).

Someone who joined by a one-time invite or who is already in, with a saved token, is never
asked.

A teammate can also join from a browser: open the web client, paste the link and type a name.
The web client shows the same session, approvals and queue as the terminal. A page served over
https can only reach a host through a relay with `wss://`; over plain `ws://` serve the web
client over http on the same network.

## Talking to the team

Not everything you type is for the agent. Two forms stay between people, and the agent never
receives them:

| You type | Who reads it |
|---|---|
| `/team lunch at one` (also `/to-team`, `/chat`) | everyone in the room |
| `@alia can you review this?` at the start of a line | only Alia, and you see your own copy |

Typing `@` lists the people in the room, `tab` completes the name. A tag inside a team message
(`/team @alia look at this`) is read by everyone and also alerts Alia. An `@` that names no one
in the room, such as `@src/app.ts`, is still a prompt for the agent.

The person you write to hears the terminal bell and sees a short message at the top right. The
browser plays a short sound and marks the tab title when the page is in the background. Turn the
bell off in `F2` → Session → *Bell on mentions*.

Participants and the host can write; viewers can read the team chat but cannot write and cannot
be written to privately. Messages are kept in the session log, so a person who was away finds
them on return, and a late joiner sees the team chat but not what was private.

<Callout type="warn" title="A private message is private from guests, not from the host">
  It is stored in the host's log, `plural-host export` includes it, and it travels through the
  relay unencrypted. Do not use it for anything you would not say with the host and the relay
  operator listening.
</Callout>
