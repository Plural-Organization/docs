title: Presence and watching a teammate
description: Share your session with the team, watch what a teammate is doing, and see when two agents touch the same file.
---

## Showing your session to the team

```bash
plural                        # in a team, this session is shared with it
plural --private              # keep this one to yourself
plural --tunnel ngrok         # shared through your own tunnel instead of the team relay
plural ls                     # who shares what right now
plural ls --json
```

Once you are in a team (`team create` or `team join`), every session you start is shared with
it, and the top bar says `◆ Backend`. `plural --private` starts one that the team does not see;
the bar then reads `this session is private`. Outside a team nothing is shared. A shared session
needs an address others can reach, so it uses the team relay, `--relay`, `--public-url` or a
tunnel. The team sees your name, the repository and branch, the title, whether the agent is
working or waiting for you, and how many people watch.

<Callout type="warn" title="A teammate sees everything in the session">
  A teammate who opens the session sees everything in it as a viewer, prompts and replies
  included, so use `--private` for work you do not want shown. The card goes away when you
  quit, or about a minute after your machine drops off.
</Callout>

The side panel has a SPACES section: your own session first, then the team's, refreshed every
few seconds: `●` working, `◆` waiting for its host, `○` idle, with who, repository and branch,
and `👁 2` when two people watch.

**Click a teammate to open their session** in the main area. You see all of it, live, as a
viewer, and your own session keeps running in the background: a click on `you` takes you back,
and `ctrl+w` closes the space you are in. What you type goes to the session you are looking at;
`alt+←` and `alt+→` remain available for moving between words in the prompt. If your own agent
needs you while you are away, its row turns `◆ waiting`, the bell rings and the terminal title
says so.

## Watching a teammate

```bash
plural join @alia            # opens alia's shared session, you can read (or click her in the panel)
plural join @alia:web        # when alia shares more than one, say the repository
```

You come in as a viewer: you see the whole conversation and what the agent does, you cannot
prompt or answer approvals. Nobody has to let you in, because the team already vouches for you,
and your name is the one in the team. The host sees you in the list of participants and in the
team card (`👁 1`), and can remove you with `/kick alia`. A host that removed you keeps you out
for the rest of that session even though you are still in the team. A host asks the team again
whenever you come back, so someone removed from the team loses their seat within about half a
minute.

If the host's relay is not the one the team uses, nothing changes for you: the card carries the
address to connect to.

## When two agents work on the same file

A team session also tells the team which files its agent changed in the last half hour and
which repository it is in (the remote address without any credentials). When two people in the
same repository changed the same file, both of them are told, nothing is blocked:

- your row in the side panel says `⚠ alia also changed src/auth.py` and hers is marked `⚠`, and
  the conversation gets a note and a short message when it first happens;
- `plural ls` prints the same line under both sessions;
- when your agent asks to edit or write a file a teammate changed in the last half hour, even if
  you have not touched it yet, the approval card says
  `⚠ src/auth.py was also changed by alia 3 min ago on feat/x`, so you can wait, ask her, or go
  on.

Only edits and writes by the agent count, not commands it runs in the shell, and only files
inside the workspace. A session that is not a team session shares nothing.
